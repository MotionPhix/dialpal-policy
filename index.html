Privacy policy — DialPal

Developer: Ultrashots  ·  Last updated: 19 October 2026

The short version

DialPal keeps your information on your phone. There is no account, no server, and no network access. Nothing you enter is sent to us or to anyone else.

What the app can access

  • Contacts — names and phone numbers, and only after you grant permission. It is read on the device so you can pick someone to stay in touch with. It is never uploaded.
  • Notifications — so a reminder can reach you.
  • Alarms & reminders (Android) — optional. Without it reminders still arrive, just later; the app tells you when that is the case.

DialPal does not request internet access. It holds no network permission, so it cannot transmit anything even in principle. This is enforced by Android, not merely promised here.

What is stored, and where

On your phone, in the app's private storage — which other apps cannot read:

  • the people you add, and how often you want a nudge about each;
  • what happened after each reminder (spoke, tried, unavailable, skipped, deferred);
  • the line you wrote about a call, and whether you marked that conversation as needing a follow-up;
  • your settings, such as quiet hours.

The one way information can leave the device

You can export a backup. That produces a readable JSON file and asks you where to save it. The destination is your choice, and from that point the file is yours — wherever you put it, it can be read by anyone who obtains it. DialPal does not send it anywhere.

Android backup

DialPal excludes itself from Android cloud backup, so its database is not copied to Google Drive. Transferring to a new phone during setup is allowed, which moves the data directly between your own devices.

Notifications

A reminder can show a person's name and the line you wrote. If you would rather it did not, turn off Show names in notifications. When that is off, the notification says "someone" instead, hides the remembered line too, and is marked secret so its contents do not appear on the lock screen.

What DialPal does not do

  • No analytics, advertising, crash reporting, or tracking.
  • No third-party SDK that transmits your information. The app's dependencies are a local database, contact and notification access, date/time handling, and the dialer handoff.
  • No account, no sign-in, no user profile.
  • No selling or sharing of information, because none is collected.

Children

DialPal is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone, of any age.

Deleting your information

Removing a person stops their reminders and takes them off your list. Their past conversations are kept, so the history you built does not develop holes; those entries appear as "Removed contact". A removal can be undone from the message that appears when you do it.

Deleting a person permanently — the second choice in the same menu — removes the person, their reminders and every conversation you recorded with them. That cannot be undone.

Uninstalling DialPal deletes everything it stored. Backup files you exported are outside the app — delete them wherever you saved them.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the revised version is published at the same address.

Contact

DialPal is developed by Ultrashots. Questions about this policy, or about how the app handles your information, can be sent to the developer contact address published on DialPal's Google Play listing.

That address is the one Google shows on the listing and the one Play sends policy and data-deletion enquiries to, so there is a single place to write to rather than two that can drift apart.
